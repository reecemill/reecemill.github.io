Can a model forecast a dangerous close approach two days out?

Satellite operators get a stream of updates before two objects pass close in orbit. Two days before closest approach, they have to decide whether a threat is real. This page compares a simple rule, which assumes the latest risk won't change, with a gradient-boosting model, on real anonymized ESA data.

Why the simple rule is hard to beat

A quarter of events get no new updates after the two-day mark. For those, the latest risk two days out is the final risk, so the rule can't be wrong. Every one of its mistakes happens on the other events.
