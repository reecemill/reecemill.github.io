Satellite Collision Risk Prediction

In progress

Satellite operators receive a stream of Conjunction Data Messages in the days before a predicted close approach, each one a refreshed collision estimate. The decision they face is expensive in both directions: a manoeuvre burns fuel and shortens mission life, while ignoring a real threat can cost the spacecraft.

Forecasting the final collision risk from data available at least two days out, using real anonymised operational records from ESA missions. The difficulty is not the model — it is that dangerous events are rare, the costs of the two error types are nowhere near equal, and the naive “carry the last estimate forward” predictor is genuinely hard to beat.

Orbit Determination & Trajectory Prediction

Planned

Public catalogue data tracks more than 50,000 objects in Earth orbit, but the published element sets degrade in accuracy the further forward they are propagated — and how fast they degrade depends on orbit regime, solar activity, and the object itself.

Modelling that error growth directly: propagating orbits, quantifying how prediction error accumulates over time, and detecting manoeuvres from orbital changes that physics alone does not explain.
